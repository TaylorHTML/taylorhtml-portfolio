GALLERYPAL

A museum guide for people who want the story—not a lecture.

GalleryPal lets visitors recognize an artwork and choose the kind of context they want, without stepping out of the gallery moment.

01The challenge

Museum visitors needed artwork context without interrupting their visit.

Museum visitors often want more than a title and date, but traditional labels, tours, and web searches can overwhelm the moment instead of opening it up.

THE PROBLEM

Visitors can enjoy a work while still feeling they are missing the artist's intent, technique, or cultural context. Finding that information usually means reading a dense label or leaving the gallery experience to search online.

THE CONSTRAINT

The solution had to be useful in seconds, work while standing in front of the art, and give control back to the visitor rather than becoming another guided tour.

HOW MIGHT WEHelp museum visitors understand and connect with art—without disrupting the visit?

02Understand

Research pointed to brief context and optional depth.

Interview and survey synthesis, including a conversation with an experienced museum educator, revealed that the best guide is concise, optional, and aware of the space around it.

01

Context creates connection

A short story or surprising detail can turn a passing glance into a memorable encounter.

02

The phone cannot become the exhibit

Information has to be brief, legible, and easy to leave so attention returns to the artwork.

03

Curiosity is personal

Some visitors want technique. Others want intent, history, or one useful fact. The interface must let them choose.

04

Pacing belongs to the visitor

A self-guided experience should support the museum visit without imposing the structure of a formal tour.

DESIGN THESIS

Give people one clear doorway into the artwork, then let them decide how far to go.
03Ideate

I explored scanning as the shortest path to artwork insights.

The sprint centered on the second an artwork is detected. If that transition felt slow, confusing, or too dense, nothing after it would matter.

06Test + learn

All three testers completed the flow. Two wanted Browse mode.

Think-aloud testing validated the scan-to-insight experience while exposing a need the original sprint had not designed for: accessing art away from the artwork.

Usability testing / 3 museum visitors

  • 3 of 3completed the core flow.
  • 2 of 3asked for a Browse mode.

What worked

  • Scan-to-insight felt immediate

    All three participants responded positively to recognizing a work and receiving context in place.

  • Tabs made depth optional

    Participants understood the content categories and liked choosing between description, technique, intent, and context.

What to improve

The experience needed a Browse mode

Two participants wanted to revisit artworks later or preview the collection without standing in front of a piece.

Next step: Add a Browse mode so visitors can preview the collection, revisit a favorite, and use GalleryPal when scanning is not practical.

07Reflection

Next: add browsing and test scanning inside the gallery.

GalleryPal reinforced that the interface is only one part of the experience. Lighting, crowds, accessibility preferences, and the emotional pull of the artwork all shape what “simple” really means.

01

Build Browse mode

Turn the strongest testing request into a second path through the collection.

02

Test in the gallery

Validate scanning in dim light, crowded rooms, and at different viewing distances.

03

Expand access

Explore transcripts, low-audio environments, and non-camera ways to identify a work.
